# Owner-Matched Evidence Beats Black-Box Early Warning

National cyber early-warning systems that work, and the AI threat-intelligence platforms that analysts trust, all solve PS-01 with the same move. They match outside evidence to one owner's specific asset, then push a typed, deadline-bound action whose score can be explained. The UK's Early Warning service does this for **13,178 organisations**, CISA's pre-ransomware calls reach victims **in under an hour**, and Korea's 2026 attack-surface pilot and Japan's DAEDALUS work the same way. Broadcast indicator sharing (CISA AIS lost **93%** of its volume) and opaque "AI domes" have failed or cannot be checked. Commercial leaders (Recorded Future, Google, Microsoft, ThreatConnect) have settled on rule-and-evidence scoring that keeps severity, confidence and relevance as separate numbers, and they limit LLMs to grounded explanation. Research agrees: models of 8B parameters or fewer are poor at open-ended ATT&CK and CVSS work, while fine-tuned security encoders are strong at it. Exploitation now usually comes before disclosure: the mean time-to-exploit is **−7 days**, and **23–29%** of exploited CVEs are exploited on or before disclosure day. So a prototype's early warning has to come from pre-disclosure sensor surges, fast ingestion of evidence and exact exposure matching, not from a predictor alone. Our own cached data shows this. **CVE-2026-104935 entered KEV the day it was published**, and CISA added SSVC decision points to it within **11 minutes**. Yet EPSS scored the chained, KEV-listed **CVE-2026-111357 at 0.3%** hours before KEV listed it. Local tests put Foundation-Sec-8B-Instruct at **65% product accuracy, 40% CWE accuracy and a CVSS mean absolute deviation (MAD) of 1.81**. It also misstated the affected version range and produced Cyrillic text in Hindi. The blueprint therefore gives the model only closed-set, span-grounded extraction and cited narration. SecureBERT 2.0 and CVE-LMTune encoders, a leakage-safe LightGBM predictor, a deterministic version-range reconciler and an SSVC tree make the decisions, and IndicTrans2 handles Hindi and Marathi. An event-sourced evidence ledger feeds a multiplicative risk score, a separate Admiralty-graded confidence, and an SSVC decision mapped to CERT-In's 12h/1d/3d/5d deadlines. The demo shows this on the real three-CVE Ivanti chain, escalating from Watch to High, Critical and then Suspected compromise.

### CISA's narrow, criteria-driven products work; its broad indicator pipe failed

CISA runs the best-documented national stack, and its own record splits winners from losers. The **Automated Indicator Sharing (AIS)** programme pushed high-volume indicators with no owner context. Indicator sharing **fell 93% from 2020 to 2022**, federal indicator collection fell about 96%, and CISA paused outreach without explanation ([FedScoop on OIG-24-60](https://fedscoop.com/cisa-threat-information-sharing-dhs-oig-report/)). Participants dropped from **304 to 135** ([The Record](https://therecord.media/cisa-pledges-to-resolve-threat-sharing-program-issues-oig-report)). The products that work are narrow and tied to actions. The **KEV catalog** admits a CVE only with "reliable evidence" of exploitation and "a clear remediation action". Failed attempts against honeypots count as exploitation; scanning and proof-of-concept code do not ([CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities)). **Vulnrichment** writes three SSVC decision points (Exploitation none/poc/active, Automatable yes/no, Technical Impact partial/total) into each CVE record's ADP container. It never overwrites the CNA's own data ([cisagov/vulnrichment](https://github.com/cisagov/vulnrichment)). **BOD 26-04** (June 2026) replaced the single KEV deadline with risk-based urgency built on asset exposure, KEV status, exploit automation and technical impact ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/cisa-directs-federal-agencies-to-prioritize-security-patches-based-on-risk/); [Qualys BOD 26-04](https://docs.qualys.com/en/etm/latest/cisa_bod/bod_overview.htm)). The cached KEV file shows the result. CVE-2026-104935 and CVE-2026-111357 got **3-day due dates**, while the authenticated, chained CVE-2026-118527 got **14 days**. Each entry also carries a new `forensicTriage` flag ([local KEV snapshot](../data/raw/kev.json)). The pre-ransomware initiative (PRNI) is the clearest "warn before damage" product: more than **4,300 notifications** since late 2022, delivered "in less than an hour" and mostly the same day the tip arrives ([SANS](https://www.sans.org/blog/cisas-pre-ransomware-notification-and-you)). The Ransomware Vulnerability Warning Pilot got **852 of 1,754** warned owners to act, about 49% ([ExecutiveGov](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/cisa-offers-update-on-ransomware-vulnerability-warning-pilot-program)). CISA's AI use is conservative, and that conservatism is instructive. A **decision-tree model** scores AIS indicator confidence, NER flags personal data, and unsupervised learning triages "terabytes of daily sensor log data". The only generative-AI drafting tool (DHS-2335) is RAG whose output staff "validate and edit before publication". Generative-AI "CTI feed correlation" is still at **initial conception** ([CISA AI Use Cases](https://cisa.gov/ai/cisa-use-cases)). Sensors alone also fail without analysts behind them. After a contract lapsed in July 2025, CyberSentry data kept flowing but went unreviewed ([CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/contract-lapse-leaves-critical-infrastructure-cybersecurity-sensor-data-unanalyzed-at-national-lab/)).

### The UK, Korea and Japan register assets and push matched alerts

The UK's **Early Warning** service is the closest national analogue to PS-01. It filters the millions of events NCSC receives each day against the IP addresses and domains that each organisation registers. It issues three typed alerts: Incident Notification, Network Abuse, and Vulnerability/Open Port. Each finding has a lifecycle ("Marked as fixed", "Risk accepted"), and cadence depends on type: immediate in the portal, a daily email for compromise, weekly for exposure ([MyNCSC service description](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/myncsc-help-centre/service-descriptions/early-warning)). It reaches **13,178 organisations** and alerted on **316,343 IPs** in 2024–25. NCSC reports outcomes, not volume: **50% of takedowns in under an hour**, against about four hours a year earlier ([NCSC Annual Review 2025](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/ncsc-annual-review-2025/chapter-02-resilience-at-scale/active-cyber-defence)). Meanwhile the forum-style sharing platform CiSP was **decommissioned on 28 November 2025** ([NCSC CiSP](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/cisp/home)). In August 2026 Korea's KISA reached the same design independently: a pilot "Attack Surface-Tailored Threat Alert Service" that monitors each SME's registered IPs, domains and software, because generic sharing made firm-specific threats hard to spot ([Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/technology/2026/08/06/kisa-launches-tailored-cyber-threat-alert-service-for-firms)). Japan's NICTER is the purest sensor-led design. About **280,000 darknet addresses** captured **~701 billion packets** in 2025, and about **55% of that traffic was research scanning**, which a detector has to suppress ([NICTER 2025 report](https://csl.nict.go.jp/report/NICTER_report_2025.pdf); [NICT press release](https://www.nict.go.jp/press/2026/02/05-1.html)). DAEDALUS turns the darknet into owner alerts by telling an organisation when its own IPs appear as darknet sources, aggregated per source IP ([NICT journal](https://www.nict.go.jp/publication/shuppan/kihou-journal/journal-vol63no2/journal-vol63no2-03-04.pdf)). The weak point is attribution of exposed assets to owners. CISA's 2021 subpoena campaign got **38 of 67** identified owners to mitigate ([CISA factsheet](https://www.Cisa.Gov/sites/default/files/2023-01/CY2021_Admin_Subpoena_Summary_Factsheet_FINAL.pdf)). Censys finds about **70% of ~134,000 exposed ICS hosts** sit on consumer or mobile networks whose owners cannot be identified ([Censys](https://censys.com/?p=11187)). A prototype that starts from a known asset inventory has already solved the hardest step in the national pipeline.

### The EU, Germany and Australia treat standards and distribution as the product

The EU's design is federated and regulatory. The Cyber Solidarity Act (in force since 4 February 2025) creates interconnected Cyber Hubs that will use "Artificial Intelligence (AI) and data analytics". No technical description of those hubs is public ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-solidarity)). The **EUVD** offers three views: critical, actively exploited, and EU-coordinated ([Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/enisa-launches-euvd)). It is missing **more than 50,000 CVEs** from its main API, a warning against relying on any single source. The Cyber Resilience Act makes "early warning" a legal term: **within 24 hours** of a manufacturer learning of active exploitation, followed by notification at 72 hours and a final report at 14 days ([CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-enisa-cve-root-dual-governance-20260518/)). Germany's BSI publishes machine-readable CSAF advisories through a well-known provider endpoint ([BSI security.txt](https://www.bsi.bund.de/.well-known/security.txt)). Australia's CTIS shows that distribution matters more than volume. It grew to **more than 550 partners** and over **1 million shared IOCs** in 2025–26 ([ASD ACTR 2025-26](https://www.cyber.gov.au/publication/annual-cyber-threat-report-2025-26/programs)) after adding Sentinel and Splunk plug-ins that move intelligence "at machine speed" ([GovTech Review](https://www.govtechreview.com.au/content/gov-security/news/asd-and-splunk-launch-ctis-plug-in-1323326616)).

### Israel and Singapore publish AI ambitions, not mechanisms

Israel's Cyber Dome is publicly described as using "generative AI platforms to help filter out genuine threats from the sea of threat intelligence and cyberattack claims" ([The Defense Post](https://www.thedefensepost.com/2023/10/17/israel-developing-cyber-dome)). Analysts note "there are no definite details about the mechanisms and tools" ([MP-IDSA](https://www.idsa.in/publisher/from-iron-dome-to-cyber-dome-defending-israels-cyberspace/)). Its one published outcome metric is **2,304 targeted alerts** in 2025 ([Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/u0gajxyo8)). Singapore buys AI capability through hyperscaler partnerships, including a Google memorandum on "AI-driven threat intelligence sharing" ([CRN Asia](https://www.crnasia.com/news/2025/cybersecurity/csa-singapore-announces-partnerships-with-google)). The one idea worth copying from both is a "claimed vs verified" label on hacktivist or chatter-derived claims. Their lack of transparency is a pattern to avoid in front of judges, not to emulate.

### India has strong reach but thin machine-readable correlation

CERT-In's scale is large. In 2025 it handled **29.44 lakh incidents** and issued **1,530 alerts, 390 vulnerability notes and 65 advisories**. Its AI-driven situational-awareness system processed **9,800 billion DNS queries** and mitigated **3,044 phishing sites**. The Cyber Swachhta Kendra covers **98% of India's digital population** ([PIB Backgrounder, Jan 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jan/doc2026123764501.pdf)). The same document describes an "automated cyber threat exchange platform", but the notes found no public format, membership or volume for it. The 1,530 alerts are published as human-readable pages that every entity must map to its own assets by hand, which is exactly the triage gap PS-01 targets. The policy layer has become concrete and time-bound. The 2022 Directions require incident reports **within 6 hours** and 180-day log retention ([PSA Legal](https://psalegal.com/new-cert-in-directions-overview-and-implications/)). The May 2026 AI blueprint, **CIGU-2026-0002**, sets patch windows of **12 hours for known-exploited internet-facing flaws, 1 day for critical external or exploited internal, 3 days for critical internal high-value systems, and 5 days for high severity**. It is advisory, not mandatory, and critics fault it for giving every sector the same guidance ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/05/223-cert-in-releases-blueprint-for-defending-against-ai-assisted-cyber-threats/)). Sector regulators add more clocks. RBI's 2026 Directions require a 24x7 SIEM-backed C-SOC, threat-intelligence feed integration, sharing through IB-CART, and reporting **within six hours on DAKSH** ([RBI](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13643)). The DPDP Rules add a **72-hour** report to the Data Protection Board ([iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/dpdp-rules-2025-operational-compliance-guide-for-indian-businesses/)). NCIIPC defines six CII sectors: Power & Energy, BFSI, Telecom, Transport, Government, and Strategic & Public Enterprises. It has no named public early-warning platform ([Wikipedia: NCIIPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Critical_Information_Infrastructure_Protection_Centre)).

C-DAC, the likely source of the judges, already sells the architecture PS-01 describes. Its **Cyber Threat Management System** places honeypot sensors that emulate an organisation's attack surface. They feed a central server with "analysis engines for threat prediction", "continuous attack surface enumeration and risk calculation", "an integrated early warning system", "India-specific cyber threat intelligence", SIEM integration and "a single dashboard". The product page names **no AI technique** and no STIX or ATT&CK standard ([C-DAC CTMS](https://www.cdac.in/index.aspx?id=product_details&productId=CyberThreatManagementSystem)). C-ADAR adds ATT&CK TTP mapping, IoC extraction and a REST API ([C-DAC C-ADAR](https://www.cdac.in/index.aspx?id=product_details&productId=C-DACAttackDatasetandAnalysisRepository%28C-ADAR%29)). C-DAC says its honeypots are "deployed across the country" and feed a central repository ([C-DAC Honeynet](https://www.cdac.in/index.aspx?id=service_details&serviceId=HoneynetTechnologies)). Indian vendors converge on "exploitability, impact, and attacker behaviour rather than severity score alone" ([CloudSEK Nexus](https://www.cloudsek.com/ar/platform/nexus-ai)) and a threat graph with vector memory ([Cyble Blaze](https://cyble.com/blog/predictive-cybersecurity-cyble-blaze-ai/)), but **none publishes its formula**. India's incident record shows the gap is correlation, not data. At AIIMS, a Zimbra flaw known since early 2022 and poor network segmentation were flagged after the 2022 ransomware attack ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2023/02/223-cyber-attack-aiims-rajya-sabha/)). The RedEcho grid intrusions were found by a foreign firm, not by Indian agencies ([ORF](https://www.orfonline.org/research/operation-sindoor-at-one-taking-stock-of-india-s-cyber-preparedness)). One core-banking supplier, C-Edge, took down payments at about **300 small banks** ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/3480250/over-300-indian-banks-suffer-payment-disruption-from-ransomware-attack.html)). Most awareness material exists only in English ([APAC News Network](https://apacnewsnetwork.com/2025/08/quick-heal-bhashini-collaborate-to-offer-cybersecurity-and-privacy-awareness-in-22-indian-languages/amp)).

## Industry platforms explain scores with evidence ledgers

### Commercial leaders separate severity, confidence and relevance

Recorded Future's score is the easiest of the market patterns to copy. Each entity type has named risk rules. The most critical rule that fired sets the base score (Very Malicious 90, Malicious 65, Suspicious 25, Unusual 5), and each rule carries an evidence string, so the explanation simply *is* the list of rules that fired ([Recorded Future](https://www.recordedfuture.com/products/browser-extension/)). Integrations show a compact "4/52" RiskString of rules triggered out of rules possible ([Google SecOps SOAR](https://docs.cloud.google.com/chronicle/docs/soar/marketplace-integrations/recorded-future)). Its AI Sessions feature is deliberately scope-limited to the Intelligence Graph ([Recorded Future AI datasheet](https://assets.recordedfuture.com/Datasheets/2026_0313%20-%20Recorded%20Future%20AI.pdf)). Google separates the questions most cleanly. The verdict says whether an indicator is bad, the severity says whether you should care, and a published table maps verdict × severity to a 0–100 threat score. A separate confidence score sits alongside, and a Threat Score Explainer acts as an "open ledger of evidence" in which signals expire and "age off" ([GTI Indicator Score](https://gtidocs.virustotal.com/docs/google-threat-intelligence-indicator-score)). Relevance comes from a configurable Threat Profile, plotted as relevance against global frequency, with a Gemini "Tell me why?" explanation ([GTI Threat Profiles](https://gtidocs.virustotal.com/docs/manage-threat-profiles)). Google also **recommends keeping Medium-tier rule packs non-alerting** to control alert volume ([Google SecOps ATI](https://docs.cloud.google.com/chronicle/docs/detection/ati-prioritization)). Microsoft bands reputation by how many ML rules match: three or more matches means Suspicious ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-xdr/entity-page-threat-intelligence)). Its Threat Intelligence Briefing Agent researches threats against the customer's own vulnerabilities. Its inputs are top-N vulnerabilities, look-back days, region and industry, and it shows a visible activity map of the steps it took ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/security/threat-intel-briefing-agent)). Palo Alto settles conflicting verdicts deterministically: the source with the highest **Admiralty** reliability wins, and a tie takes the worse verdict ([Cortex XSOAR](https://cortex-docs.paloaltonetworks.com/cortex-xsoar-8-saas/investigate-and-respond-to-threats/threat-intel-management/indicator-investigation/indicator-verdict)). The agentic XDR vendors publish agent rosters but **no accuracy figures** ([CrowdStrike](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/crowdstrike-fall-2025-release-defines-agentic-soc-secures-ai-era)).

### TIPs and open-source platforms supply the guardrails and the data model

ThreatConnect's CAL score documents the guardrails a robust score needs: bounded influence per signal, diminishing returns for repeated observations, a fast rise with slow decay, capped activity amplification, and false-positive feedback counted as a risk-reducing signal ([ThreatConnect CAL](https://knowledge.threatconnect.com/docs/cal-global-threat-score)). Cyware computes confidence as a weighted average of relations, enrichment, source confidence and sightings ([Cyware CTIX](https://techdocs.cyware.com/ctix/en/ctix-confidence-score-engine.html)). Anomali blends an ML score with each source's own reported confidence ([Anomali](https://docs.anomali.com/Content/Features/threat_model/Observables/Observable_Confidence.htm)). EclecticIQ supports **self-hosted LLMs via Ollama for air-gapped deployments**, which is a direct precedent for a sovereign laptop stack ([EclecticIQ 3.5](https://docs.eclecticiq.com/ic/3.7.2/release/3.5/)). Feedly flags "CVSS > 8, or > 5 with a known exploit" and uses ML to forecast CVSS for unscored CVEs ([Feedly](https://feedly.com/new-features/posts/track-emerging-threats-with-feedly-ai)). Flashpoint's summaries are "reference-backed" ([Flashpoint](https://flashpoint.io/news/flashpoint-unveils-ai-summarization-for-search-and-investigations-at-black-hat-usa-2025/)). Among open-source platforms, MISP contributes the decay model `score(t) = base × (1 − (t/τ)^(1/δ))`. Its base comes from Admiralty and other taxonomy tags, a new sighting resets it, and a per-type lifetime τ sets how fast it ages ([MISP training](https://www.misp-project.org/misp-training/a.5-decaying-indicators.pdf); [MISP blog](https://www.misp-project.org/2019/09/12/Decaying-Of-Indicators.html)). MISP also uses warninglists of known-benign values ([misp-warninglists](https://eugit.opencloud.lu/MISP/misp-warninglists/src/commit/14edcbd433a6a15c5723975b6e438f66d91b45bf)). OpenCTI keeps the indicator *score* (default 50, aged by decay rules) separate from entity *confidence* and Admiralty reliability ([OpenCTI](https://docs.opencti.io/latest/usage/reliability-confidence/)). Its deployment needs more than 16 GB of RAM across Elasticsearch and the core, so on a laptop running an LLM the right move is to copy its data model, not deploy it ([OpenCTI deployment](https://docs.opencti.io/latest/deployment/overview/)). STIX 2.1 maps Admiralty credibility 1–5 to confidence 90/70/50/30/10 ([python-stix2](https://stix2.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/confidence/stix2.confidence.scales.html)).

### Vulnerability prioritisation has moved from severity to evidence plus context

Severity alone is a poor triage tool. CVSS ≥ 7 covers **50.7% of CVEs** for 74.6% coverage of exploited ones, while EPSS v4 reaches the same coverage at **6% effort** ([Empirical Security, EPSS v4](https://research.empiricalsecurity.com/research/introducing-epss-version-4)). EPSS v3 was XGBoost with SHAP explanations, a one-month gap between training and test windows, and PR-AUC **0.78 against 0.05 for CVSS** ([arXiv 2302.14172](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.14172)). EPSS v5 (2026) mainly improved probability calibration ([EPSS V5](https://research.empiricalsecurity.com/research/epss-v5-is-here)). EPSS is reactive, though. It crossed 0.10 before KEV listing for only **19%** of KEV bugs since 2023, and KEV came first for **57%** ([Barracuda](https://blog.barracuda.com/2026/07/24/KEV-gap-how-fast-do-exploited-bugs-get-flagged)). Commercial vulnerability-management tools all multiply a threat score by asset context. Qualys TruRisk multiplies by a 1–5 asset criticality, applies **×1.2 for external assets**, and gives KEV, malware and threat-actor evidence more than 90% of the weight ([Qualys TruRisk](https://docs.qualys.com/en/vm/latest/mergedProjects/search_in_vmdr/threat/calculating_asset_risk_score.htm); [Qualys QDS](https://docs.qualys.com/en/vm/latest/mergedProjects/risk_score/risk_score/measuring_likelihood_of_vulnerability_exploitation.htm)). Kenna scores an asset as its worst vulnerability × priority, **+200 for an external IP** ([Kenna](https://help.kennasecurity.com/hc/en-us/articles/4402070116116-Understanding-Vulnerability-Asset-and-Risk-Meter-Scoring)). SSVC turns this into a decision (Track, Track\*, Attend, Act) ([CISA SSVC](https://www.cisa.gov/stakeholder-specific-vulnerability-categorization-ssvc)). CVSS v4 treats an undefined Exploit Maturity as **Attacked**, the worst case ([FIRST CVSS v4.0](https://www.first.org/cvss/v4.0/specification-document)). Research has moved to time-valid rankers judged at a fixed analyst budget. ThreatLens recalls **80% of future KEV entries in its top 20**, about three times EPSS, and warns that retrospective rankings "overstate performance" ([arXiv 2608.22306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22306)). Expected Exploitability raised precision from 49% to 86% ([arXiv 2102.07869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07869)). LLMs asked for SSVC decision points **over-predict risk** and reach at best "fair agreement" ([arXiv 2510.18508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18508)). Exploitation timing makes evidence ingestion mandatory. Mean time-to-exploit fell to **−7 days in 2025** ([Help Net Security on M-Trends 2026](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/03/24/mandiant-m-trends-2026-report/)). **72% of 2023 CVEs with a public PoC were never known exploited** ([GTIG](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/time-to-exploit-trends-2023)). **29% of 2026 KEV CVEs were listed on or before their NVD publication day** ([Ujeebu](https://ujeebu.com/data/cisa-kev-days-from-cve-publication-to-exploited)). Since April 2026, NVD enriches only KEV, federal-use and critical software, so CNA and ADP data must fill the gap ([NIST](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/04/nist-updates-nvd-operations-address-record-cve-growth)).

### Sensor grids provide the only signal that leads disclosure

GreyNoise's 2026 study counted **104 surge events, of which 68 preceded a vendor-matched CVE, with a median lead of 11 days**. It found session volume to be the primary signal and sessions plus unique IPs together to be the strongest. Distributed surges led by **21.3 days** and surges from concentrated hosting by 7.5 days ([GreyNoise](https://www.greynoise.io/blog/the-internet-changes-before-the-advisory-drops)). Its product alerts on a rolling 2-hour window against a user-set threshold ([GreyNoise Event Feeds](https://www.greynoise.io/blog/introducing-vendor-cve-and-tag-spike)). These are vendor-run studies with unpublished thresholds. The noise model is well documented elsewhere. In one ISC case, six IPs produced about **20 billion** honeypot hits ([ISC diary 32100](https://isc.sans.edu/diary/32100)). More than half of RCE attempts come from previously unseen IPs ([GreyNoise State of the Edge](https://www.greynoise.io/press/greynoise-releases-2026-state-of-the-edge-report)). Shadowserver sends daily reports to **201 national CSIRTs** using a 38-field honeypot schema ([Shadowserver 2025 review](https://www.shadowserver.org/news/shadowserver-2025-highlights-of-the-year-in-review/); [schema](https://www.shadowserver.org/what-we-do/network-reporting/honeypot-brute-force-events-report/)). Speed after disclosure is now extreme. An AI-orchestrated campaign against PaperCut got first RCE about four hours after setup and compromised 78 organisations in its first hour ([GreyNoise](https://www.greynoise.io/blog/ai-orchestrated-campaign-against-papercut-ng-mf)). Amazon's MadPot honeypots saw exploitation **4 days before disclosure** ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/amazon-threat-intelligence-identifies-php2shell-exploitation-prior-to-public-disclosure-in-madpot/)). Forecasting that holds up uses structured external telemetry: Liu et al. predicted breaches at 90% TP / 10% FP ([USENIX 2015](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity15/technical-sessions/presentation/liu)). Twitter features did not beat a text baseline when the study was replicated ([arXiv 1707.08015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.08015)). RITA-style beacon scoring (Bowley skew, MADM, connection density) is cheap and explainable ([Active Countermeasures](https://activecm.github.io/threat-hunting-labs/beacons/)).

### AI research says encoders map, retrieval grounds, and LLMs explain

Open models of 8B or fewer are weak at open-ended CTI work. On AthenaBench, Qwen3-8B scores **11.8% on ATT&CK technique extraction and 5.5 F1 on mitigation recommendation**, against GPT-5's 76% and 32.6 ([AthenaBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01144v2)). The best of seven open models reached a micro-F1 of only **0.22** on ATT&CK classification of full reports ([arXiv 2606.18166](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.18166)). LLMs on CTI are "inconsistent and overconfident" ([arXiv 2503.23175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23175)). Fine-tuned encoders do far better on structured tasks. The CVE-LMTune SecureBERT classifier reports a micro-F1 of **0.8168** over 175 ATT&CK sub-techniques ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/Sana9/securebert-vuln2attack-flat)). SecureBERT 2.0 reports NER F1 **0.945** and retrieval R@1 **88.83%**, against 77.87% for MiniLM ([arXiv 2510.00240](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00240v1)). A 125M-parameter RoBERTa matches Foundation-Sec-8B-Reasoning on CVE→CWE (**75.6% vs 75.3%**) ([arXiv 2603.14911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14911)). On published CTIBench numbers, Foundation-Sec-8B-Reasoning leads the 8B class (RCM 0.753, ATE 0.491) ([arXiv 2601.21051](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21051)), and retrieval narrows gaps more than model scale does ([CTIArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11974v1)). The literature has settled on linear pipelines of narrow steps, in which building relationships is the weakest stage ([arXiv 2607.16175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16175)). CTI text is attacker-influenced input: polluted feeds are a named injection vector for AI SOCs ([arXiv 2609.10707](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10707)).

## What works and what fails, across every class

Seven patterns recur in the systems that succeed. First, they **match evidence to an owner**: NCSC Early Warning, KISA's pilot, DAEDALUS and Shadowserver all push asset-matched findings, never a raw feed. Second, they rely on **narrow, criteria-driven authoritative signals**. KEV's three-part inclusion test and SSVC's three decision points are trusted because their criteria are public. Third, they **score transparently and attach evidence**: Recorded Future's fired rules, Google's evidence ledger, Microsoft's rule counts and ThreatConnect's Impact Factors all let an analyst answer "why 72?". Fourth, they **keep classical ML in the decision path and the LLM behind a human gate**, as CISA does. Fifth, they **lead with sensors**: darknet and honeypot surges come before disclosure, and nothing else reliably does. Sixth, they **distribute into tools defenders already run** (ASD's SIEM plug-ins, BSI's CSAF endpoint). Seventh, they **measure outcomes** (NCSC's takedown time, PRNI's sub-hour notification, RVWP's 49% action rate).

The failures mirror these. AIS shows that **a firehose without owner context or confidence** loses its participants. CyberSentry shows that **sensors without analysis capacity** produce nothing. EUVD's 50,000-CVE gap and NVD's 2026 enrichment cutback show that **one source is never enough**. Cyber Dome and the agentic-SOC marketing show that **unpublished AI cannot be trusted or checked**; Forrester's verdict is that AI on weak intelligence is "advanced weaponry loaded with rusted ammunition", and buyers should "demand transparency instead of accepting AI as a black box" ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/announcing-the-forrester-wave-external-threat-intelligence-service-providers-q3-2026/)). **CVSS-only triage** wastes about eight times the effort. **EPSS-first triage** lags KEV most of the time. **Free-form LLM output of ATT&CK IDs or mitigations** fails benchmarks badly. India's own incidents add one more failure: **the signal existed but nobody correlated it to an owner in time**.

The single most important cross-cutting finding is that the bottleneck has moved from data to decisions. **91% of CISOs value CTI, but only 26% say it significantly influences their decisions**, which SANS calls "a translation problem, not a credibility one" ([Flare on SANS 2026](https://flare.io/learn/resources/blog/sans-cti-survey-2026)). Practitioners' top complaints are judging accuracy (50%), information overload (46%) and lack of context for their own environment (46%) ([Cybersecurity Dive](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/threat-intelligence-customer-complaints-recorded-future/805300/)). A prototype stands out by turning each warning into a decision: an owner, a deadline, a cited reason. Being more "intelligent" than competitors matters less. Two further insights follow. Government practice is **more conservative than vendor marketing**, so a glass-box design is the safer pitch to C-DAC. And because CISA itself lists generative-AI feed correlation only as a concept, a **working, validated LLM-assisted advisory-to-asset correlation** is credibly ahead of published government practice, provided it is shown with its error rates.

Foundation-Sec's product misses follow a pattern: they are about **granularity, not comprehension**. It returned a component or a description where the CPE product name was needed: "NRF component" for Open5GS, "pinctrl" for the Linux kernel, "workflow automation platform" for n8n, "Atomik theme" for Concrete CMS ([local bake-off](../docs/bakeoff_results.json)). Its near-parity on vendor confirms it understands the text. Turning product extraction into a choice among candidates drawn from the CPE dictionary therefore attacks the actual error. Rule (a) bars qwen3:8b from core roles, so this structural fix is how the blueprint tries to recover the roughly 30-point gap. That recovery stays unproven until the bake-off is re-run.

The CWE result, **40% locally against 70.4% published for the same Instruct model** ([arXiv 2601.21051](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21051)), suggests published CTIBench numbers overstate accuracy on new CVEs. Q4 quantisation, a zero-shot prompt and n = 40 could all contribute, so the blueprint sends CWE to an encoder and re-measures. Per-metric CVSS accuracy was poor: Foundation-Sec got Integrity right on only 12.5% of CVEs and Privileges Required on 32.5%. That matches CTIBench's 1.31–1.91 MAD range for LLMs ([CTIBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07599v3)). The LLM must never set a CVSS score.

The narrative test exposed a subtler hazard. For CVE-2026-104935, Foundation-Sec wrote "versions up to and including 25.1.3.0". The Ivanti CNA record lists 25.1.3.0 as **unaffected**, and it also lists **22.8R2.5** as the fixed release on the 22.8 branch, which both Foundation-Sec and qwen3:8b omitted ([local samples](../docs/bakeoff_samples.md); [local CVE record](../data/raw/vulnrichment/CVE-2026-104935.json)). Its Hindi output contained Cyrillic fragments ("произ") inside Devanagari sentences ([local samples](../docs/bakeoff_samples.md)). Version strings, fixed releases and translations therefore cannot come from this model.

The cached feeds also contain a ready-made, real demo story, along with three engineering traps.

**Speed of authoritative evidence.** CVE-2026-104935 was published at **15:31:07Z on 13 October**. CISA's ADP container recorded SSVC **active / yes / total** at **15:42:16Z**, and KEV listed it the same day with a 16 October due date ([local CVE record](../data/raw/vulnrichment/CVE-2026-104935.json); [local KEV](../data/raw/kev.json)). The predictor has zero lead over KEV here. Early warning on this CVE can only come from a pre-disclosure surge and from instant exposure matching.

**Prediction lag.** CVE-2026-111357 (CVSS 7.0, AV:L) was published on 15 October. Its cached record says "as exploited in the wild in October 2026" and cites Volexity's 13 October write-up on chained zero-day exploitation. EPSS (`model_version v2026.06.15`, consistent with the v5 release) still scored it **0.00316** at 12:03Z on 30 October. KEV listed it at 17:02Z that day with a 2 November due date ([local EPSS](../data/raw/epss_scores-current.csv.gz); [local CVE record](../data/raw/vulnrichment/CVE-2026-111357.json)). A third chained CVE, CVE-2026-118527, entered KEV on 30 October **before any NVD or EPSS record existed** in the snapshot. That is exactly the case where the system must score from KEV and CNA text alone.

**Version-range sources disagree.** The simulated inventory has four Ivanti Connect Secure gateways ([local world](../data/sim/world.json)):

- **PWR-01 and PWR-02** run 22.8R2.4. Every source says they are affected.
- **BFS-01** runs 25.1.3.0. Every source says it is unaffected.
- **TEL-01** runs 22.7R2.12, and here the sources split:
  - A strict reading of the CNA block (`defaultStatus: affected`, with 22.7R2.14 listed only as one unaffected version) marks it **affected**.
  - NVD's CPE configuration does not include it, so it is **unaffected**.
  - The description says the flaw "does not impact ICS version 22.7R2.14 or prior", so it is **unaffected**.

Getting this right takes multi-source reconciliation plus span-grounded extraction of the "or prior" clause. Matching on vendor name gives two false positives.

**Format mismatch.** The inventory encodes `connect_secure:22.8r2.4` in the CPE version field, while NVD encodes version `22.8` with update `r2.4`. `packaging.version` cannot parse "22.8R2.4", so the matcher in PLAN.md would fail silently. The fix is vendor-scheme comparators.

## Blueprint: models extract, evidence decides, analysts approve

### Eight layers, one append-only evidence ledger

The architecture follows the five-layer national pattern (sensors → central repository → enrichment → constituency filtering → notification) and C-DAC's own CTMS vocabulary, with an AI correlation layer added. Every derived number is a pure function of an append-only evidence ledger plus versioned configuration. That makes the system dynamic: every new event triggers a re-score, and the early-warning timeline is simply the alert's score history. It also makes the system reproducible.

```
L0 SOURCES  connector plugins, cached offline; each source tagged Admiralty A–F and max TLP
   CVE JSON 5 + CISA ADP (Vulnrichment) · NVD 2.0 · CISA KEV · EPSS · ATT&CK STIX · CTID PKEV
   Feodo · OpenPhish · CERT-In-style advisories [SIM] · decoy grid [SIM] · fw/DNS/auth logs [SIM]
   asset inventory + org/supplier graph [SIM]
        │ append-only events
L1 NORMALISE  → STIX 2.1 / CVE5 / CPE 2.3 · defang · warninglists · PII redaction · spotlighting
        │
L2 EXTRACT & ENRICH  deterministic first → encoders second → LLM last, closed-set, span-grounded
   regex IOC/CVE · version-range reconciler · SecureBERT 2.0 NER / bi-encoder / cross-encoder
   CVE-LMTune CWE + ATT&CK · Foundation-Sec-Instruct extraction (candidates in, spans out)
        │
L3 DETECT  surge (sessions + unique IPs, robust z) · beacon (RITA) · IsolationForest
           IOC sightings · KEV/NVD trend bursts · HDBSCAN campaign clusters
        │
L4 CORRELATE  graph: Advisory–CVE–CPE–Asset–Org–Sector–Supplier–IOC–Campaign–Technique
              chain detection · supplier fan-out · blast radius / choke points
        │
L5 SCORE & DECIDE  pure function(ledger, config), re-run on every event
   named rules (crit 1–4) → evidence floor · LightGBM p̂ (calibrated, SHAP) · E × I × R × K
   Admiralty confidence (decay, independent origins) · SSVC tree → tier · India SLA + clocks
        │
L6 EXPLAIN & ACT  deterministic alert card (primary) · validated Foundation-Sec narrative
   patch (CNA fixed versions, KEV requiredAction) · block (IOC, STIX) · investigate (ATT&CK, forensicTriage)
   CERT-In 6 h draft · STIX 2.1 bundle (machine claims as Opinion) · CSV blocklist · IndicTrans2
        │
L7 PRESENT  FastAPI + SSE · Situation Room · alert card · timeline · graph · decoy grid · replay
X  role registry + eval gates · config store · provenance/audit · TLP gating · offline guard
```

Connectors follow the IntelOwl and OpenCTI plugin split: external import, enrichment and export. Export applies a `maximum_tlp` gate ([IntelOwl](https://github.com/certego/IntelOwl)). Adding CERT-In, MISP or a TAXII collection therefore means writing one plugin that emits ledger events. The core does not change.

### The scoring formula is multiplicative, gated and glass-box

PLAN.md's additive formula, `Risk = 100 × (0.30E + 0.25A + 0.25I + 0.20V)`, produces inversions that a government judge can expose in one question. A flaw nobody exploits (E = 0) on a crown-jewel asset with strong evidence scores **70, which is High**. A KEV-listed flaw on a low-tier internal lab box scores about **78, also High**. Evidence V is counted twice, once inside Risk and again as Confidence, and "Severity = mean(E, I)" mixes likelihood with impact. Every vendor and government precedent instead multiplies likelihood by impact by asset context and keeps confidence separate: Google's verdict × severity with a separate confidence, OpenCTI's split of score from confidence, Qualys's criticality × 1.2 external multiplier, Kenna's max × priority. The blueprint therefore produces five separate outputs from the ledger.

**1. Rule ledger and evidence floor (Recorded Future pattern).** Named rules fire from ledger events. Each has a criticality and emits an evidence string with its source letter and date. The highest-criticality exploitation rule sets an evidence floor, exactly as Recorded Future's base score works.

| Criticality | Exploitation rules | Floor | Context rules (shown, no floor) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | In CISA KEV; SSVC Exploitation = active from an A/B source; own-decoy exploit-payload tag on ≥2 sensors (KEV counts honeypot attempts) | 1.0 | Chained with a KEV CVE on the same asset |
| 3 | "Exploited in the wild" claim extracted with a span from a CNA, vendor or CERT text; C/D-source claim (shown as "claimed, unverified") | 0.8 | National-advisory mention; predictor in top 1% |
| 2 | Public PoC / SSVC = poc (Mandiant: 72% of PoC'd CVEs are never exploited) | 0.5 | EPSS ≥ 0.5; vendor surge on the decoy grid |
| 1 | — | 0 | CVSS ≥ 9; edge device; Automatable = yes |

The card shows "k/N rules fired". An Unusual or criticality-1-only alert never pushes a notification, following Google's non-alerting Medium tier and Recorded Future integrators raising the Suspicious threshold from 5 to 25.

**2. Exploitability.** `E = max(floor, p̂)`. Here p̂ is the LightGBM probability of KEV listing within 365 days, calibrated isotonically on the validation fold. EPSS appears on the card as external corroboration and fires a context rule, but never enters p̂. Qualys's >90% evidence weight and CVSS v4's Attacked default both say hard evidence should override any prediction. The cached EPSS score of 0.003 for KEV-listed CVE-2026-111357 shows why.

**3. Impact.** `I = 1.0` if SSVC Technical Impact = total. Otherwise I is the normalised CVSS 3.1 impact subscore, `[1 − (1−C)(1−I)(1−A)] / 0.915`, using the CNA or ADP vector. Assets flagged as OT safety assets get `I ≥ 0.9`. The LLM never supplies I.

**4. Relevance and asset context.** `R` = 1.0 when the reconciled version range contains the asset's normalised version; 0.6 for a product match with an unknown or unparseable version; 0.3 for vendor-only or sector-targeting matches; **0 for "cleared"**. A cleared match is still shown, as negative evidence with its reasons. `K = (C/5) × X × B`, where:

- C (1–5) is criticality, with 5 meaning NCIIPC CII or crown jewel.
- X is exposure: **1.2** when the asset is internet-facing *and* the vulnerability is network-reachable, either directly or through a chained AV:N CVE on the same asset; 1.1 for DMZ; 1.0 for internal.
- B (1.0–1.3) is a blast-radius factor for choke-point assets on a path to tier-1 systems.

**5. Risk, confidence and decision.**

- **Risk**: `Risk = min(100, 100 · E · I · R · K)`. It orders the queue and rolls up to org and sector by max, the Kenna pattern.
- **Confidence** is computed separately. Each evidence item gets a MISP-decayed weight, `w = r_source × (1 − (Δt/τ_kind)^(1/δ))`, with τ set per kind: KEV never decays; sensor surges last about 14 days; IOC sightings about 7. Items are then grouped by **independent origin**, so CISA's KEV entry and CISA's ADP record count as one vote. Origins combine by noisy-OR, `1 − Π(1 − max w per origin)`. Confidence is displayed as an Admiralty pair (for example B2) and exported as STIX confidence (1 → 90 … 5 → 10). This gives the bounded influence, diminishing returns and slow decay that ThreatConnect specifies, and the Admiralty conflict rule settles any disagreement between sources.
- **Decision** comes from a config-defined, CISA-aligned SSVC tree. Its inputs are Exploitation (from the floor), Automatable (CISA ADP, or a CVSS proxy of AV:N ∧ AC:L ∧ PR:N ∧ UI:N until ADP arrives), Technical Impact, Exposure (including chains) and Mission (C ≥ 4 or CII). Outcomes are Act → **Critical**, Attend → **High**, Track\* → **Medium** and Track → **Watch**. Two overrides sit on top. An IOC sighting or beacon from an asset with an exposed vulnerability becomes **Suspected compromise** (NCSC's alert type), which starts the incident clocks. "No fix available" becomes a compensating-control state, following KEV's remediation criterion. Pushed alerts require Critical or High *and* confidence of at least medium. Medium goes to a daily digest and Watch stays on the dashboard, following NCSC's cadence.

Because the tier comes from the tree and not from score thresholds, every tier has a readable decision path. The number orders alerts and the path justifies them.

**Worked example (PWR-01 VPN, Ivanti 22.8R2.4, internet-facing, CII power, C = 5):**

- **13 Oct, 15:31Z** (CVE-2026-104935 published). I = 0.80 from the CNA vector C:H/I:L/A:L. R = 1.0 and K = 1.2, so **Risk = 96·p̂**. The tree, with Exploitation none, Automatable yes by proxy and Mission high, gives Attend → **High**.
- **15:42Z** (CISA ADP active/yes/total, then KEV). E = 1.0 and I = 1.0, so Risk = min(100, 120) = **100**. Decision Act → **Critical**. Confidence: CISA (A) plus the Ivanti CNA (B), two origins.

TEL-01 and BFS-01 show **R = 0, "cleared"**, each with its source votes. The leaf values of the tree must be checked against the CISA SSVC Guide before the demo, because the notes did not retrieve them.

### Each role has one model, one contract and one eval gate

Rule (b) forbids fallback models, so each role has exactly one assigned model. If a role's output fails validation, the narrative is marked **withheld**. Nothing substitutes another model. The deterministic alert card is the primary rendering, always present, and the LLM narrative is an optional layer on top of it. This replaces PLAN.md's "template fallback" and its "model missing → next model". Swapping a model is a config change followed by a re-run of that role's eval gate (model-agnostic by contract), and the model digest is stamped on every output.

This assignment respects rule (a). It also replaces `qwen3-embedding:0.6b` in [config/models.json](../config/models.json) with the SecureBERT 2.0 bi-encoder, because the generic embedder is neither security-specialised nor benchmarked here, while SecureBERT 2.0 reports R@1 of 88.83% against MiniLM's 77.87%. CIRCL's VLAI severity classifier (>82% accuracy) is an optional addition for CVEs without any CNA/ADP vector, such as CVE-2026-118527 ([arXiv 2507.03607](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03607v1)).

The VRAM plan follows from 6.9 GiB free. One Foundation-Sec Q4_K_M (about 4.9 GB) stays resident with an 8k q8 KV cache. The Reasoning model cannot co-reside, so it runs in batch with `keep_alive 0`, ideally during the 03:00–05:00 sleep slot. Encoders, IndicTrans2 and all standard ML run on the CPU-only torch build. At the measured 3.83 s per call, extracting all ~560 CVEs a day would take about 36 GPU-minutes. A deterministic vendor prefilter against the inventory cuts that to minutes.

Prompt-injection hygiene is structural, because Foundation-Sec has no dedicated safety alignment ([arXiv 2601.21051](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21051)). The model gets no tools and no write access. Untrusted text is wrapped and labelled as data, following spotlighting ([arXiv 2403.14720](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14720)). Output is constrained to a JSON schema. Every ID is validated against local catalogues. Above all, risk is computed outside the model, so injected text cannot change a score.

### The leakage-safe predictor is judged at an analyst budget

The exploit predictor should copy EPSS's protocol and avoid four traps in PLAN.md. First, a 365-day label window means CVEs published after October 2025 are **right-censored** as of the 30 October 2026 KEV snapshot, so PLAN's test window to April 2026 is invalid. Train on CVEs published from January 2021 to June 2024, leave a three-month gap, and test on October 2024 to October 2025. Second, drop NVD reference tags such as "Exploit", which are added after publication, and drop mutable NVD metadata, as ThreatLens does. Third, never use EPSS as a feature, and never compare against *current* EPSS: today's scores contain look-ahead information, and correcting look-ahead bias is "first-order" ([IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/documents/how-good-epss-really-five-year-empirical-evaluation-correcting-look-ahead-bias)). Fourth, judge it at a budget: recall in the top 1% and 2%, effort against coverage versus CVSS ≥ 7 and ≥ 9, calibration, and the distribution of days from publication to KEV listing. That distribution must disclose the day-0 share, about 29% for 2026 CVEs. Features are CVSS vector components taken from CNA or ADP data, CWE groups, capped vendor/product, an edge-device flag, description keywords and TF-IDF-SVD, and the vendor's KEV count as of the CVE's publication date.

## Risks and unverified claims

The biggest technical risk is **rule (a)'s accuracy cost**. qwen3:8b's 95% product accuracy is off the table, and closed-set selection has not yet been shown to lift Foundation-Sec above its 65%. If it fails the gate, the deterministic matcher must carry products alone. That is safe, but it weakens the "AI extraction" story. **Published model numbers are self-reported.** That covers SecureBERT 2.0's 0.945 and 88.83%, CVE-LMTune's 0.8168 (its data extends to 17 June 2026, so tests on earlier 2026 CVEs leak), and Foundation-Sec's CTIBench scores, which our 40% CWE result already contradicts. The quantisation loss is unmeasured. **GreyNoise's surge-precedes-CVE result** is vendor-run with unpublished thresholds and no independent replication, and the demo's surge is simulated. Present it as a calibrated simulation, not a finding. The claim that **CVE-2026-111357's "exploited in the wild" text** existed at publication is unproven, because the CNA container was updated on 28 October. Check the cvelistV5 history before claiming a 15-day lead over KEV.

Several anchors in the notes are secondary or unverified:

- The SSVC deployer-tree leaves were not retrieved from the CISA SSVC Guide.
- The CERT-In blueprint deadlines come from MediaNama, not from the CERT-In text.
- The CIVN field format and the Directions' incident-type picklist were not fetched from cert-in.org.in, so label the CERT-In draft as "Directions-aligned".
- The NCIIPC sector list comes from Wikipedia.
- C-DAC's nationwide honeypot feed to CERT-In rests on a single news source.
- BOD 26-04's details conflict between sources. The local KEV due dates of 3 and 14 days partly reconcile them.
- EPSS v5's headline metric is unnamed. The cached file's `v2026.06.15` tag suggests v5 is now public, but this is unconfirmed.

Operational risks:

- Foundation-Sec's tool-calling support is unverified. The JSON-query-plan copilot avoids depending on it.
- IndicTrans2's quality on security text is unmeasured.
- The Reasoning model may not be pulled yet.
- VRAM figures are estimates.
- The hackathon's ban on "unauthorized assistance" still needs organiser confirmation for AI coding tools.
- KEV is a conservative, US-centric label set, and India has no machine-readable exploited-vulnerability feed to substitute.
- All n = 40 local results carry intervals of roughly ±15 points.
- With about 21 hours left, the P0 cut should be the reconciler, ledger, scoring, alert card and the Ivanti replay. The predictor, surge detector, beacon detector and IndicTrans2 come next.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "AI early warning" should mean for this build. The world's most effective systems are not the most intelligent. They are the most *accountable*. Every warning resolves to an owner, a version-exact exposure, a cited reason and a statutory clock. AI earns its place by doing the unglamorous correlation that turns CERT-In's 1,530 human-readable alerts into per-asset decisions. Against our own cached data, the prediction-first framing in PLAN.md does not survive. The flagship CVE was in KEV on disclosure day, and EPSS was still near zero for its exploited sibling. What stands out instead is evidence ingestion that is faster than the attacker, exposure matching that is more exact than the vendor's own CNA record, and a score whose every factor a C-DAC scientist can audit.

The deeper opportunity is that this design fills gaps the Indian ecosystem itself has documented: sector-agnostic guidance, supply-chain concentration, English-only dissemination, and alerts with no machine-readable correlation. C-DAC's CTMS already names "early warning", "risk calculation" and "India-specific CTI" but publishes no AI technique. A prototype that shows a validated, offline, glass-box AI layer on top of that pattern, with REAL and SIM events labelled honestly, offers the judges a believable next version of their own product rather than another dashboard.
